## Digital perm technique — textbook facts

Note on sourcing: **no English cosmetology textbook found uses the term "digital perm."** The reliable technical descriptions come from (a) Japanese trade publishers and salon-industry media, (b) the equipment maker that owns the trademark, and (c) Wikipedia's summary. Each bullet is attributed.

**Definition and mechanism**
- A digital perm applies perm lotion, winds the hair on **rods heated by a machine with a digital temperature readout**; heat sets the shape. Japanese salons market it as 形状記憶パーマ ("shape-memory perm"). — [Hot Pepper Beauty Magazine](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/); [Wikipedia: Digital perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_perm)
- Stylists commonly just call it a **"hot perm"**; the name *digital perm* is a registered Japanese trademark of **株式会社パイモア (Paimore Co., Ltd.)** of Sakai, which holds the device patent and trademark registrations 第4740678号 and 第5150727号. Strictly, only Paimore equipment may be called デジタルパーマ. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_perm); [Paimore](https://paimore.com/products/index12.html); [Sakai IPC interview](https://www.sakai-ipc.jp/showcase/ipc38_interview1/)
- Chemically: cold perm = **reduction lotion + neutraliser only**; digital perm = **lotion + applied heat**, which raises shape-memory and gives a stronger, longer-lasting curl. — [Paimore](https://paimore.com/products/index12.html); [Costa Hair guide](https://www.costa-hair.jp/column/mens-perm-hajimete-complete-guide-2026-04-26)
- Hot perming is generally run with an **acidic** lotion plus heat; cold perming with an **alkaline** lotion and no heat. — [Act Point Salon](https://www.actpoint.com.sg/comparing-cold-perms-and-digital-perms/)

**The single most practical difference — wet vs dry**
- **Cold perm: wave is strongest when the hair is WET and slackens as it dries. Digital perm: wave is strongest when the hair is DRY and slackens when wet.** The digital perm therefore mimics a curling-iron/hot-roller finish. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_perm); [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/)
- Consequence for daily routine: with a digital perm you **must blow-dry it out** to see the wave — towel-dry, work in treatment, dry root-to-tip fully, finish with a light wax. With a cold perm you scrunch product into damp hair and let it dry. — [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/)

**Which hair it suits**
- Digital perm is recommended for **stiff/coarse hair and high hair density — hair that "won't take" an ordinary perm.** That describes typical thick straight East Asian male hair. — [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/)
- It is **contraindicated on repeatedly bleached hair and on hair that has had 縮毛矯正 (Japanese straightening)** — risk of ビビリ毛 (frizzed, over-processed "buzzed" hair). — [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/)
- Cold perm is the better choice for **loose waves / natural curl**; digital perm for **firm curls, roll curls, and くびれ (cinched) silhouettes**. — [Costa Hair](https://www.costa-hair.jp/column/mens-perm-hajimete-complete-guide-2026-04-26)
- Conflicting claim worth flagging: some English salon sources say digital perms are gentler and better for *fine* hair, and cold perms better for coarse hair — the opposite of the Japanese sources. Treat the Japanese framing as authoritative for Asian hair. — [Act Point Salon](https://www.actpoint.com.sg/comparing-cold-perms-and-digital-perms/)

**Duration**
- Japanese consumer-facing: **about 2–3 months**, with regrowth at the roots requiring maintenance. — [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/)
- Salon technical guide: **cold 2–4 months, digital 4–6 months, spiral 3–4 months.** — [Costa Hair](https://www.costa-hair.jp/column/mens-perm-hajimete-complete-guide-2026-04-26)
- (These disagree; the honest range is ~2–6 months depending on hair condition and how short the style is cut.)

**Damage and cost**
- Because heat is involved, load on hair **and scalp** is higher than a cold perm — though residual chemical is said to be lower. Costa Hair rates the damage as "slightly more than cold." — [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/); [Costa Hair](https://www.costa-hair.jp/column/mens-perm-hajimete-complete-guide-2026-04-26)
- Time in chair: **~2–3 hours**. Price guide: **¥15,000–20,000** in Japan. Digital perms cost more than cold perms generally, and salons with only one machine may charge a premium because of booking constraints. — [Hot Pepper Beauty](https://beauty.hotpepper.jp/magazine/403725/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_perm)

**Aftercare**
- Blot with a towel rather than rubbing; dry with the hair pushed in the direction you want it to sit; make treatment/oil moisturising a habit. — [Costa Hair](https://www.costa-hair.jp/column/mens-perm-hajimete-complete-guide-2026-04-26)
- Curl that has slackened during the day can be revived by finger-curling — no re-wetting or re-product needed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_perm)

**Interaction with pushed-back / slick-back / center-part styling — the important caveat**
- **A digital perm cannot be taken close to the roots**, because the heated rods would risk burning the scalp. It is therefore **poorly suited to styles that need root lift / ふんわり立ち上げ at the front** — which is exactly what a pushed-back or volumised center part depends on. A **cold perm**, which uses lotion alone and can sit nearer the scalp, holds root volume better and keeps curl when dry with a springy body. — search summary of Japanese stylist sources (see Sources table, query 22)
- Practical reading: digital perm = mid-lengths and ends, flow, C-curve, cinched silhouette on medium hair. Root lift for a kakiage/pushed-back front = cold perm, a separate root perm, or iron work. The two are often combined.
- For center part + perm specifically, Japanese stylist guidance is that a **loose** perm reads soft/gentle and a **strong** perm reads wild — pick the strength to the impression you want. — [Ozmall center-part×perm guide](https://www.ozmall.co.jp/hairsalon/men/centerpartperm/)
- Pro-book support for the root problem: ヘアモード社's メンズパーマのヘアカタログ 2021-2022 documents **ダウンパーマ / 骨格補正パーマ** as separate named techniques used to *control* form and volume at the roots and sides — i.e. the profession treats root-zone work as its own operation, not part of the digital perm. — [Hair Mode](https://hairmode.jp/product/books/jm19558/), [メンズ韓国ヘアBOOK](https://hairmode.jp/product/books/jm23541/)
- Milady's English framing for the same physics: **thermal waves are "activated by an outside heat source."** No English textbook found treats root-proximity limits for heated rods. — [Milady Standard Cosmetology TOC / study material](https://www.milady.com/catalog/milady-standard-cosmetology)

**Training material (not a book, but the most detailed thing found)**
- HAIRCAMP (Japanese salon e-learning) sells a session billed as 「髪質改善デジタルパーマの完全マニュアル／教科書」 — a fully manualised digital-perm protocol taught by stylists 宮本 and 青木. Paid, streaming, Japanese only. — https://haircamp.jp/magazines/eiichi_st_live9

## Gaps

1. **No book anywhere explains digital perm vs cold perm at textbook depth.** The Japanese pro books (GOALD, Hair Mode, 髪書房) cover winding, chemical selection and volume theory, but none advertises a digital-perm chapter. The best manualised treatment found is a **paid Japanese video course** (HAIRCAMP), not a book. English cosmetology textbooks (Milady) never use the term at all — they classify by alkaline/acid chemistry and by "thermal waves." **If you want digital perm in print, the realistic answer is manufacturer (Paimore) technical literature or trade-magazine back issues, not a book.**
2. **Korean side is thin for what you want.** Korea has plenty of NCS/licence textbooks (cut, general perm theory) and one flagship pro book (남성 헤어 토탈 시스템, 2016 — now a decade old), but **no Korean equivalent of a current men's medium-long / digital-perm style catalog** surfaced. Ironically the best Korean-men's-hair *technical* book found is Japanese: メンズ韓国ヘアBOOK.
3. **TOCs unavailable** for 남성 헤어 토탈 시스템 and 헤어펌 웨이브 디자인 — could not confirm perm/digital-perm coverage for either. Would need a bookstore visit or a "미리보기" preview widget that the fetch tool couldn't reach.
4. **No 2025-26 or 2026 FINEBOYS+plus HAIR edition confirmed.** The publisher's own back-issue page stops at '24-'25 ベスト版 (July 2024). Either the series slowed or the page is stale. Rakuten Magazine / dマガジン subscriptions would show the true latest issue but require logging in — not done.
5. **No Men's Non-no hair mook exists** as far as could be determined; hair is an in-magazine feature. The free 360° web catalog is the better artefact anyway.
6. **Popeye / Brutus / smart / Samurai ELO issue-level hair features not indexed** — Magazine House's back-number pages are not searchable by feature topic through search engines. Would need manual browsing of https://magazineworld.jp/popeye/back/ issue by issue.
7. **No preview pages viewable** for any of the Japanese pro books beyond cover + a couple of publisher-chosen spreads. Page numbers for specific style names could not be obtained for any title.
8. **Face-shape / 骨格 guidance is under-served in book form for men.** The one on-target item is a section inside 男髪セレクション特別編集 (骨格補正), plus 骨格補正パーマ in the perm catalog. Standalone 骨格診断 books on the market are about clothing, not hair.
9. **Internet Archive has nothing Asian or modern.** Its hairdressing holdings are Western and mostly pre-1980 — irrelevant to digital perm or Korean/Japanese men's styling.
